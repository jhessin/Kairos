## **Project Title:** _Chronicles of the Ancient Near East_

## **Genre:**

Educational Historical MMO

## **Platform:**

PC (primary), later mobile/console

## **Camera:**

Third‑person, exploration‑focused

## **Target Audience:**

Students, churches, homeschoolers, curious gamers, history enthusiasts

## **Design Pillars:**

- **Authenticity:** Historically grounded environments, cultures, and artifacts
- **Exploration:** Player learning through discovery, not exposition
- **Cooperation:** Shared missions, archaeology digs, cultural reconstruction
- **Respect:** Non‑denominational, culturally sensitive presentation, grounded
  in Biblical accuracy

---

## **1. Executive Summary**

A massively multiplayer online world set across the cultures of the Ancient Near
East, allowing players to explore historically accurate environments, engage in
archaeology, learn languages, and experience daily life in biblical-era
societies. The game emphasizes cultural learning, values, and historical context
rather than reenacting scripture. Sessions last 20–60 minutes, with a focus on
exploration, cooperative tasks, and knowledge progression.

---

## **2. Player Promise**

Players will feel **curious**, **immersed**, and **enlightened** as they uncover
how people lived, worked, traded, worshipped, and built civilizations across the
biblical world. The game promises **discovery over combat**, **cooperation over
competition**, and **learning through interaction**.

---

## **3. Core Gameplay Loop**

1. **Explore** historically accurate regions
2. **Interact** with NPCs, artifacts, and cultural systems
3. **Complete quests** tied to archaeology, trade, craftsmanship, or daily life
4. **Unlock knowledge** entries, skills, and cultural insights
5. **Progress** through new regions, languages, and historical periods

This loop reinforces learning through repeated exposure to authentic
environments and systems.

---

## **4. Mechanics & Systems**

### **4.1 Player Actions**

- Movement: walk, run, mount animals, climb, swim
- Interaction: talk, trade, examine artifacts, gather materials
- Archaeology: dig, brush, categorize, restore artifacts
- Crafting: pottery, metalwork, textiles, agriculture
- Language learning: Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek basics through contextual tasks
- Cooperative actions: group digs, caravan missions, construction projects

### **4.2 Progression**

- **Knowledge Level:** Unlocks cultural insights, codex entries, and new regions
- **Skill Trees:** Archaeology, craftsmanship, linguistics, diplomacy
- **Reputation:** With cities, guilds, and cultural groups
- **Cosmetics:** Clothing styles from various periods (no pay‑to‑win, no micro-transactions)

### **4.3 Economy**

- **Resources:** clay, ore, grain, textiles, timber
- **Trade:** historically accurate barter systems
- **Crafting:** items used for quests, teaching, or cosmetic customization

### **4.4 Failure States**

- Failed digs (damaged artifacts)
- Incorrect cultural reconstructions
- Failed diplomacy attempts
- Environmental hazards (heat, dehydration, wildlife)

---

## **5. Controls & Camera**

- **Camera:** Third‑person, adjustable zoom
- **Controls:** Standard WASD movement, contextual interaction key, radial menu
  for tools
- **Accessibility:** Colorblind modes, simplified controls, optional auto‑navigation

---

## **6. Art Direction**

(Designed to align with potential Bible Project collaboration)

### **6.1 Style**

- Stylized realism with clean shapes and readable silhouettes
- Warm, earthy palette
- Animation: smooth, expressive, culturally respectful gestures

### **6.2 Asset Categories**

- Characters: scribes, merchants, shepherds, artisans, soldiers
- Environments: Judea, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Phoenicia, desert routes
- Props: pottery, scrolls, tools, architecture, flora/fauna
- UI: parchment‑inspired menus, iconography based on ancient motifs

### **6.3 Production Notes**

- Bible Project assets may inform character proportions, color palettes, and
  environmental stylization.
- All art must be historically grounded and non‑denominational.

---

## **7. Audio Direction**

- Ambient soundscapes: markets, livestock, desert winds, workshops
- Music: regional instruments (lyre, oud, flute, percussion)
- Voice acting: accents inspired by region, not modern stereotypes
- UI sounds: soft parchment rustles, tool clicks

## **8. World & Content Structure**

### **8.1 Regions**

- **Judea (Second Temple Period)**
- **Egypt (Late Period)**
- **Mesopotamia (Neo‑Babylonian & Persian periods)**
- **Phoenician coastal cities**
- **Nomadic desert routes**

Each region includes:

- Major cities
- Villages
- Trade routes
- Archaeology sites
- Cultural hubs (temples, markets, workshops)

### **8.2 Time Periods**

Players do not time‑travel; instead, regions represent stable slices of
historical periods. Seasonal changes occur. The initial cutscene should open
present day. There is an organization recruiting test subjects for a case study
in quantum physics. You are one of those test subjects. They are in fact looking
for people to quantum-sync with versions of themselves in an alternate reality
that is several thousand years in the past. This way the player will understand
the language of their host and be able to progress in the game.

[For more or the game's story click here](story)

### **8.3 Quests**

- Daily life quests (farming, trade, craftsmanship)
- Archaeology missions (dig, restore, catalog)
- Cultural diplomacy (resolve disputes, negotiate trade)
- Historical events (scriptural, culturally contextual)

### **8.4 NPC Systems**

- Schedules: work, rest, travel
- Roles: scribes, artisans, merchants, priests, soldiers
- Dialogue: educational, contextual, branching

## **9. Educational Framework**

### **9.1 Learning Objectives**

- Geography of the Ancient Near East
- Cultural practices (agriculture, trade, craftsmanship)
- Archaeological methodology
- Basic ancient languages
- Values: hospitality, justice, stewardship, community (Biblical values
  presented in a non‑denominational way)

### **9.2 Assessment**

- Codex entries unlocked through exploration
- Optional quizzes
- Achievements tied to cultural mastery
- Museum hubs where players display restored artifacts

### **9.3 Academic Integration**

- Optional educator dashboards
- Curriculum alignment (history, archaeology, religious studies)
- Safe mode for younger players (no combat, simplified systems)

## 15. Characters

### 15.1 Starting Classes

At the beginning of the game, players choose a starting class by selecting an
NPC role model in the opening cutscene. This choice defines their initial
skills, progression focus, and gameplay style.

#### Classes

- **Archaeologist**: Focused on excavation, artifact restoration, and cultural
  discovery. Role model is an experienced archaeologist leading the dig.
- **Bible Scholar**: Emphasizes scriptural knowledge, language learning, and
  cultural context. Role model is a learned scholar interpreting ancient texts.
- **Physicist**: Concentrates on scientific progression, alternate dimension
  research, and technology. Role model is a physicist studying historical
  phenomena through scientific methods, i.e., string theory.
- **Soldier**: Specializes in protection, combat, and security missions. Role
  model is a seasoned soldier ensuring safety of the team and sites.

### 15.2 Opening Cutscene

The opening cutscene introduces the player to these four NPC role models, each
representing a distinct approach to exploring and understanding the Ancient Near
East. The player chooses which NPC to follow, thereby selecting their class and
initial gameplay path.

This system encourages re-playability and diverse learning experiences, aligning
with the educational and cooperative pillars of the game.
